## The question

In 2026 the media says that AI makes software engineers much more productive. If this is true at company level, the revenue per person of IT services companies must increase. This study tests the claim with the published reports of four listed IT services companies: EPAM, Infosys, Cognizant and Accenture (together about 1.57 million people).

## Method

- One measure: **revenue per person** = revenue ÷ average headcount, in nominal US dollars.
- The same period two years apart: Q3 2024 and Q3 2026.
- Also: headcount, gross margin, operating margin, and the AI revenue that the companies disclose.
- The values come from the company reports. The paper lists the source of each value.

## Results

| Company | Revenue per person, 2 years | Headcount, 2 years |
|---|---|---|
| Infosys | +0.8% | +4.3% |
| EPAM | +1.5% | +20.3% |
| Cognizant | +3.8% (adjusted for the Belcan acquisition) | +5.9% |
| Accenture | +8.1% | +5.7% |

1. Revenue per person grew slowly or not at all.
2. Headcount grew at all four companies. No report shows AI replacing people at company level.
3. The two companies that publish an AI revenue share (EPAM 13%, Infosys 10.5%) show the smallest gain per person.
4. At Accenture and Cognizant, the margin gain came from lower overhead, not from delivery: their gross margin fell.
5. Three of the four companies speak about pricing pressure or about passing the AI gain to clients.
